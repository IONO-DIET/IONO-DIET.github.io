AAPG2022 · IONO-DIET · CE49 · Joint ANR–FAPESP project

Detecting earthquakes and tsunamis from space, before the waves arrive.

IONO-DIET is a 60-month France–Brazil research program developing the first near-real-time system to detect earthquakes and tsunamis from the disturbances they trigger in the ionosphere. This page reports the project's progress and outcomes to its funding agencies.

About the project

Earthquakes and tsunamis remain among the most destructive natural hazards. Earthquakes cannot be predicted, so the only way to reduce their toll is to warn faster and more accurately once one occurs. Today's tsunami warning systems rely on seismometers, seafloor pressure sensors, and coastal GNSS stations — but these methods still struggle to correctly size the largest earthquakes, and a reliable estimate of tsunami potential can take 20 to 30 minutes to produce.

The ground displacement of an earthquake launches acoustic and gravity waves that travel upward through the atmosphere. Within 7 to 9 minutes they reach the ionosphere, where they leave a measurable signature in signals from GNSS satellites — arriving well before a tsunami reaches the coast, and reaching far offshore where no coastal instrument can sense a submarine quake directly.

IONO-DIET turns that signature into a new, near-real-time channel for hazard assessment. The project is coordinated jointly by the Institut de Physique du Globe de Paris (IPGP, France) and the Instituto Nacional de Pesquisas Espaciais (INPE, Brazil), with collaborators at NORSAR (Norway) and Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University (USA).

Fundamental objective

Understand how the ionosphere responds to earthquakes and tsunamis, and how that response depends on the source and on background conditions.

Methodological objective

Build near-real-time methods to detect these events and estimate magnitude, source size, and tsunami wave height from ionospheric data alone.

Applied objective

Test the resulting system on live GNSS streams in high seismic-risk regions and learn its limits under real operating conditions.

2.1 Human resource training

Doctoral theses, postdoctoral positions, international mobility

The project funds and supervises early-career researchers in both countries. Four doctoral students defended theses related to the project (one thesis, at IPGP, was externally funded); all four continued as postdoctoral researchers; two new PhD students have since joined, and a further postdoctoral researcher has been recruited at IPGP. Exchange between the two institutions is also supported by international mobility, such as Rogério H. Honda's eight-month stay at IPGP funded by CAPES.

2.3 Software production

Operational platform and software tools

Near-real-time detection platform

TECpro

Downloads GNSS data in near-real time, runs the project's detection methods, and displays ionospheric disturbances driven by earthquakes, tsunamis, and other natural hazards on a public web page — the first ionosphere-based monitoring service of its kind.

Lead developer: Saúl A. Sanchez Juarez

Team
